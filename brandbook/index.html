01 The brand

An engineering firm that sells hours back to you

Pluscode is an AI consultancy and software house. We map how work actually runs inside a company, put AI where it removes real paperwork and manual steps, and keep it running in production. The identity has one job: to make that sound like a measurable promise rather than a pitch.

AI consulting that puts artificial intelligence to work. For real.

The headline line, and the spine of every page. "For real" always sits in emerald. It is the sentence the rest of the brand defends.

02 Voice and tone

Engineers, not slide decks

The voice is the most distinctive thing Pluscode owns. It is plain, concrete and slightly blunt: it counts hours, names processes, and says out loud where AI will not help yet. Read a sentence back and ask whether an engineer who did the work could have said it.

Concrete over impressive

Name the artefact, not the ambition. "Delivery notes that arrive as PDFs, phone photos and email attachments" beats "unstructured document ingestion" every time.

Count, do not claim

Hours, weeks, percentages, headcount. If a claim has no number behind it, either find the number or cut the claim.

Say the limit

"We honestly flag where it won't yet." Admitting the edge of the work is what makes the rest of the page credible. Never write around a limitation.

Second person, active voice

"You leave with", "you talk to the engineer who would do the work". The reader is in the sentence, doing something.

Short sentences, full stops

Fragments are allowed when they land. "For real." "Fixed scope, fixed price." Rhythm carries more conviction than length.

No em-dashes

House rule. Recast with a comma, a colon, a bracket or a new sentence. Em-dashes make copy read as though nobody chose the punctuation.

On the page

Write this
Not this
We follow the paperwork and count where the hours go. Names a physical activity and a unit of measure.
We leverage cutting-edge AI to unlock operational efficiencies. Nothing in it can be checked, priced or refused.
Start with one process, not a transformation programme. Sets a scope the reader can say yes to on a Tuesday.
Begin your enterprise-wide digital transformation journey. Sells a budget cycle, not a first move.
You do not need another digital product. You need less paperwork. Contradicts the reader's assumption, then replaces it.

Runs the repetitive steps, stops where risk starts. Describes the actual behaviour of the system.

Mechanics

Headlines
Sentence case. One idea. The emphasised phrase is set in emerald, never italic, and never longer than four words.
Eyebrows
Two or three words, uppercase, describing the band: Capabilities, How we work, Selected work. Never a sentence.
Buttons
A verb and its object: Book a discovery call, Scope a pilot, View all case studies. Never "Learn more" or "Submit".
Numerals
Digits for anything countable, including one to nine. "2 weeks", "40+ projects", "8 industries".
Spelling
British-neutral English on the site. Avoid words that split the two, and prefer the shorter form when they do.
Jargon
Allowed only where the reader already uses it. "Straight-through processing" earns its place in logistics; "RAG" does not, so we say "answers from your own documents, source attached".
Never
Exclamation marks, emoji as section markers, "revolutionary", "game-changing", "seamless", "synergy", or any sentence that would survive a find-and-replace of the company name.

03 Logo

Two discs and a wordmark

The mark is a small filled disc overlapping a larger one, with the intersection knocked out. It is drawn as a single lockup with the wordmark: the two never separate, and the wordmark is never reset in another typeface.

Primary · white on navy pluscode-logo.svg. The default lockup. Header, footer, mega menu, Open Graph cards.
Secondary · black on paper pluscode-black-logo.svg. Documents, invoices, decks, anything printed on white.

Clear space

Keep free space on every side equal to half the lockup height. At the native 203 × 44 artwork that is 22px. Nothing crosses it: no type, no rule, no photograph edge, no other logo.

Misuse

Do not tint it emerald. The lockup is white or black. Emerald is an accent colour, not a logo colour.

Do not put white on a light ground. Switch to the black lockup rather than adding a shadow to rescue it.

Do not stretch or condense. Scale both axes together, always from the supplied SVG.

Do not box or badge it. No containers, outlines, rounded plates or drop shadows.

Do not reset the wordmark. The letterforms are artwork, not live Figtree text.

Do not place it on busy imagery. If the ground is noisy, lay the navy gradient over it first, as the hero does.

04 Colour

Deep navy, cool paper, one emerald

Two grounds and a single accent. Navy carries the hero, the mega menu and the footer; a cool paper white carries everything that has to be read at length. Emerald is the only chromatic colour in the system, so it is spent carefully: roughly one emphatic use per screen.

Rules of use

One emphasis per view
A single emerald element should be doing the pulling. A second one halves the value of the first.
Pick by ground
#047857 for accent text on light, #34d399 for accent text on dark, #059669 for fills on either.
Emerald fills carry navy
Text on an emerald button is Night, not white. 4.71:1, and it keeps the button feeling like a surface rather than a label.
Atmosphere runs on alpha
Glows, grids and rings are always #10b981 at 7% to 25%. Never a second hue.
Semantics stay separate
Success, warning and error states are their own colours. Emerald is a brand accent and must not double as "success", or state stops reading as state.

05 Typography

One family, doing every job

Figtree, variable, 300 to 900, with a matching italic. There is no second face. Display, body, and the uppercase label slots that most systems hand to a monospace are all Figtree, separated by weight, size and letter-spacing rather than by family. It is a deliberately narrow constraint, and it is what keeps the pages feeling engineered rather than assembled.

Aa400 Regular

400, 500 and 600 do the work. 300 appears nowhere on the site today, and 700 and up are available but unused: if a headline needs more weight it usually needs fewer words.

Setting rules

Measure
Keep running text at 60 to 66 characters. Leads cap around 580px, body columns around 720px.
Balance
Every headline gets text-wrap: balance. A two-line headline with one orphan word is a bug.
Emphasis
Colour, not italic. The emphasised phrase in a headline is emerald and stays upright, so it reads as a highlight rather than a tone shift.
Tracking
Negative above 24px, none in body, generous in uppercase. Never letterspace lowercase text.
Numerals
Tabular figures wherever numbers stack: statistics, tables, step numbers, prices.
Fallback
ui-sans-serif, system-ui, -apple-system, sans-serif. Declared on every role so a failed font load degrades rather than reflows.

Why one family

The system keeps three token slots, --font-sans, --font-serif and --font-mono, and points all three at Figtree. Components that ask for a serif display or a mono label keep working untouched, and the site loads two font files instead of six.

If a second face is ever introduced, it goes into the serif slot for display only. The mono slot stays pointed at Figtree: the uppercase label style is doing that job already, and it is doing it better on a page this dense.

06 Layout and grid

1240 pixels and a 2 pixel radius

The page is a stack of full-bleed bands, each holding a centred 1240px column. Sections alternate cream, white and navy so the rhythm reads without dividers. Corners are almost square throughout: 2px, which is the single most recognisable structural decision in the system.

Container
max-width 1240px, centred. Editorial reading pages narrow to 760px.
Gutters
20px on phones, 40px from 640px up.
Band padding
80px vertical on phones, 100px from 640px up. Dense bands drop to 64px, never lower.
Header
72px tall, fixed, transparent until the page scrolls 8px, then navy at 90% with a blur and a 10% white hairline.
Announcement bar
40px, above the header, pushing both down. Dismissed state is remembered, so the offset is applied before first paint rather than after.
Radius
2px on every rectangle: buttons, cards, inputs, menus, chips. Fully round only for dots, avatars and icon buttons. Nothing in between.
Hairlines
1px. #e2e6ec on light, white at 8 to 15% on dark. Structure is drawn with lines, not shadows.
Shadows
Reserved for things that float: menus, popovers, the mega menu. Long, soft and heavily offset, for example 0 40px 80px -20px rgba(0,0,0,0.6). Cards never carry one.
Sticky columns
Section headings pin at 110px on two-column bands, so the eyebrow stays with the list it introduces.
Grid overlay
The blueprint grid is 56px square, emerald at 7%. It marks a band as atmospheric, so it never sits under dense reading copy.

08 Motion

One curve, four durations

Everything that moves uses the same easing: a fast start that settles long and softly. Content arrives by rising 28px while it fades in. Nothing bounces, nothing springs, nothing loops for attention.

--ease-out-expo

cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)

It covers most of the distance in the first third of the duration, then eases out for a long time. That is why long durations here do not feel slow: by the time the eye arrives, the element has almost stopped.

200ms
Micro states: dropdown chevrons, a menu panel appearing under a trigger.
300ms
The default. Every hover colour change and the 4px arrow travel.
500 to 700ms
Panels and overlays: the mega menu slides in over 700ms and back out over 500ms, because leaving should be quicker than arriving.
900ms
Scroll reveals. Opacity 0 to 1 and 28px of vertical travel, fired once when an element is 12% inside the viewport.
50ms
The stagger between siblings in a revealed group. Enough to read as a sequence, short enough that the group still arrives together.
Ambient
The status dot pulses over 2.4s. Logo marquees run 38s or 60s. Nothing else loops.
Scrolling
Lenis drives the page, so native smooth scrolling is switched off to stop the two fighting.
Reduced motion
Under prefers-reduced-motion, every duration collapses to 0.001ms and reveals render in their final state. This is enforced globally, not per component.

09 Surface and texture

Four atmospheres, all emerald

Dark bands are never flat. Four treatments give them depth, and all four are built from the same emerald at low alpha, which is why the navy never picks up a competing cast.

Blueprint grid 56px squares, emerald at 7%. Marks a band as structural: hero, contact, promo cards.
Emerald glow A radial gradient from 22% to zero at 65%, anchored off the top right corner.
Film grain Fractal noise at 0.85, two octaves, 50% opacity, overlay blend. Used sparingly on rich sections.
selected text
Selection Emerald ground with white text. A small detail, and one of the few places the brand shows up unprompted.

The animated hero gradient

The hero runs a WebGL gradient over an opaque Night base, drifting between #0a1929 and #061220. It is atmosphere, not content: it must never pull attention off the headline, and it is replaced wholesale when background media is set, because an opaque canvas would cover the media underneath.

Photography and media

Where a hero carries an image or video, two overlays go on top: a 105 degree navy gradient from 94% to 60% opacity, then a vertical fade from Night to transparent. The rule is simple: the headline has to survive the picture, so the picture is always dimmed to serve it.

A note on the token names

The accent token is called --color-lime and holds an emerald. The name is a fossil from an earlier palette and it stays, because renaming it would touch several hundred class names across the site for no visual gain. Read "lime" as "the brand accent" and the system is consistent.

The same applies to --font-serif and --font-mono, which both resolve to Figtree. The slots are kept open so a future face can be introduced in one place.
